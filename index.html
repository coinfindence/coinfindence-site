Four engines. Different jobs. One system.

This section should feel approachable. People should understand what each engine is for without needing a decoder ring or a PhD in overcomplicated trading acronyms.

Engine A

Range / Retest

A

Designed for sideways conditions and controlled retests. Aims to reduce the kind of random chop that chews up traders who cannot stop pressing buttons.

  • Made for range conditions and reaction zones
  • Focuses on lower-risk entry structures
  • Helps reduce repeated stop-outs in messy markets
Best used when price is reacting, not exploding.
Engine B1

Trend Continuation

B1

Built to follow cleaner continuation moves after a trend is already established. This is the part that tries to work with momentum, not wrestle with it for entertainment.

  • Targets structured continuation rather than late chasing
  • Uses trend context and execution timing together
  • Designed for steadier directional participation
Best used when trend structure already exists and the market offers a valid continuation.
Engine B2

Momentum Expansion

B2

Built for acceleration phases. If the market shifts from orderly movement into real expansion, this engine is designed to catch that change without turning into chaos.

  • Designed for breakout and expansion conditions
  • Fits faster moves and sharper directional release
  • More aggressive than B1, but still rule-based
Best used when price is no longer drifting and starts to push with force.
BMV Module

Opportunity Engine

🔓

A higher-frequency opportunity module built for traders who want more valid setups. It is meant to add opportunities, not to turn the chart into a vending machine.

  • Adds short-term opportunities under valid conditions
  • Extends activity without throwing away discipline
  • Advanced module intended for unlocked plans
Best used by traders who want more activity after understanding the core system.

Decision Panel

See the context before taking the trade

HTF bias, regime, market score, and risk state are shown together so traders can decide whether the trade deserves attention at all. That matters more than one more entry arrow.

  • Higher timeframe directional context
  • Regime awareness built into the screen
  • Market score for fast decision support
Risk Control

Built-in discipline, not after-the-fact excuses

Time Stop logic can flag trades that consume time without producing enough progress. That means less dead weight, fewer low-efficiency positions, and a cleaner workflow.

  • Time Stop to reduce low-efficiency holds
  • Helps avoid getting stuck in stale setups
  • Supports selective participation over constant exposure

Is this suitable for beginners?

Yes, if they want a cleaner, more guided workflow. The page and system should explain enough to be approachable without removing useful depth.

Does this predict the market?

No. It is an execution framework, not a crystal ball in a premium subscription wrapper.

What does BMV mean for the user?
